Project Atlas

One graph for your systems, and everything left to do to them.

Most teams keep a description of their systems in one place and the list of work against them in another, and the two drift apart within a week. Atlas holds both as one graph: what exists, how it's organised, and every open piece of work bound to the part of it that changes — at whatever level of detail you need, from the whole system down to one small part of it.

Map what you're actually running

Build a picture of your systems piece by piece, as deep as it needs to go, and keep it as one live structure instead of a document that only describes it. See what's healthy and what needs attention at a glance, or drop into one part and see nothing but what's relevant there.

Track work against any part of it

File work as epics, stories and tasks, each bound to the part of the system it actually changes. Closing a task updates the story above it, the epic above that, and the system it all belongs to — automatically, with no second board to keep in sync by hand.

See only what's yours

A personal view pulled from the same graph everyone else works from: every open item assigned to you, wherever in the system it lives, without having to go looking for it project by project.

State a purpose once, everywhere it applies

Attach a durable statement of intent, a constraint or a rule to any part of the system, and every piece underneath it inherits it automatically. Say it once at the right level instead of repeating it on every child that needs to know.

Share what makes sense, keep the rest private

Open a system to everyone you work with, or keep it private and hand out access one person at a time. Access follows the structure, so granting a whole system grants everything inside it in one step.

Let an agent work the same graph you do

Give an automated agent its own credential instead of sharing yours. It sees exactly what the person behind it is allowed to see — never more — and reads and writes through the same interface a person would, so nothing it does happens out of sight.

Know who changed what, and when

Every change to the graph and every sign-in is recorded, so you can look back at the history of a system or a piece of work without having to ask around for it.

Who it's for

Anyone running more than one system who is tired of the description living in one place and the to-do list living in another. Atlas is built for a single person or a small team who want one place that shows both what exists and what's being done about it — not a wiki next to a ticket tracker, one graph doing both jobs.
